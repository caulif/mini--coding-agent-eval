mini coding agent eval

Exploring agent evaluation,
from concepts to real experiments

After changing a coding agent's system prompt, does it actually get better at fixing code? I wanted a project small enough to inspect, yet substantial enough to retain real problems. These are my ideas, design choices, and experimental results.

Overview

Answering that question means defining “better” and collecting evidence. I start with evaluation goals, evidence, and grading, then carry those choices into a task set, executable code, and an A/B comparison.

01–04Define the decisionGoals, evidence, and criteria05–08Build the measurementTasks, records, and comparisons09–10Implement the designFrom choices to runnable code11–12Interpret the evidenceObservations and next steps

The experiment did not show a repair benefit from the new prompt. It did expose problems with response budgets, verification claims, and missing judge verdicts. Those findings are the starting point for further work. The code, tasks, and original records are included for inspection and further exploration.
